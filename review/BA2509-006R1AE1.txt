Please find below the comments by one reviewer:


A clear and useful paper. It tightens the theory connecting simulation-based calibration (SBC) and binary prediction calibration (BPC) for Bayes-factor validation, shows where data-averaged posterior (DAP) checks and the “Good check” fall short, and offers a practical workflow (including posterior SBC) that works on real examples. I recommend the authors to improve the paper by focusing on clarity, reporting details, and a couple of extra comments.

1. You recommend “hundreds” to “thousands” of simulations (Sec. 7.3), and figures show power curves, but readers could use rules of thumb: e.g., for noise in log-BF with sd is about 2 (Fig. 3A–C, p.18), how many sims for 80% power at \alpha=0.05? Consider summarizing these as a small table.

2. You discuss loss of exact equivalence between SBC and BPC beyond K=2 (Sec. 3.2). A short experiment with K=3 (e.g., nested GLMs) comparing (i) one-vs-rest BPC, (ii) top-class calibration, and (iii) SBC on model index would strengthen the generality claims.

3. You mention loss of power from random tie-breaking for discrete quantities (Sec. 7.1–7.2 allude to it). A brief ablation (e.g., mid-ranks or jittering) would be informative.

4. You argue for frequentist tests over the Bayesian t-test and mention Gaffke’s test; it would help to standardize on one recommended DAP test (with code snippet) so users don’t have to choose. (Sec. 3.5)

5. The “turtles” probit GLMM and BayesFactor examples are great. If space allows, adding one continuous-outcome hierarchical model (e.g., Gaussian LMM with non-conjugate priors) would broaden external validity.
